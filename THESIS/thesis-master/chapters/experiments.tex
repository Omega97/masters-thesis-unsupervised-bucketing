\subsection{Base NNUE}\label{sec:res-base}

We also tested the soft cross-entropy of the base NNUE model as a function of the size of the dataset, from 50k samples, all the way to 50 million. The model has a \(2\times 128\)-neuron accumulator layer, and a 256-neuron L2 layer, which lays on the larger side of the models we trained.

\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\ttfamily\footnotesize,breaklines=true]
python3.12 -u scripts/train_nnue-gpu.py --epochs 100 --lr 0.01 --lr-end 0.001 --hidden-dim 128 --hidden2-dim 256 --test-fraction 0.01 --batch-size 10000 --batches-per-epoch 512 --train-val-subset-size 10000 --run-name dual_h128_H256_e100_bpe512_bs10000 --plot plots/dual_h128_H256_e100_bpe512_bs10000.png
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{variable_dataset_size_ce.png}
\caption{Soft cross-entropy of the base NNUE as a function of training-set size.}
\label{fig:nnue-ce-vs-size}
\end{figure}

We found that, below 2M positions, the model clearly overfits the training set. On the other hand, above 3M positions no overfitting is visible. These figures are to be kept in mind when partitioning a dataset for the MoE, as the per-model data should never exceed this threshold.
