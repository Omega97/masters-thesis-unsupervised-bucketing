% Topic 
In the context of resource-constrained board game engines, a common approach for improving model performance is to partition the state space and allocate distinct experts to each region. While this is the central idea behind \emph{Mixture of Experts} (MoE) architectures, manually defining the partition often requires domain-specific expertise and may yield suboptimal results. 

% What we do: NNUE + LLM techniques: MoE, sample-gradient partitioning. (L1 clustering intentionally omitted for now)
We investigate and compare several approaches to extending the \emph{Efficiently Updatable Neural Networks} (NNUE) paradigm beyond its conventional formulation, with a particular focus on techniques that have been successfully explored in other areas of machine learning but remain relatively uncommon in NNUE-based chess engines. Mixture-of-Experts architectures are a natural extension for chess evaluation that allows multiple specialized heads to share a common NNUE representation. We also explore sample-gradient-based partitioning, using the gradients of individual training samples to identify groups of positions with similar learning requirements, an approach more commonly studied in large language models and other deep learning settings. Finally, in an effort to make the NNUE architecture more applicable to other fields, we experiment with making its conventional accumulator a learned world model of the game.

% compare to L1 clustering, sample-grad, world models
The results highlight different trade-offs between the investigated approaches. L1-based clustering combined with a Mixture-of-Experts architecture improves evaluation performance, while introducing only a minor increase in memory usage and inference cost; the lightweight dispatcher accurately reproduces the learned partition. In contrast, sample-gradient-based partitioning is difficult to exploit at inference time, since the gradients depend on the unknown true value of the position, and a lightweight dispatcher struggles to approximate the resulting complex partition. 
%%Finally, the world-model experiment explores an alternative to the conventional NNUE accumulator and demonstrates that part of the resulting architecture can be trained without direct supervision from a teacher engine or game outcomes, although the unsupervised objective can reduce evaluation strength.
Finally, we explore an alternative to the conventional NNUE accumulator based on a learned world-model encoder. We additionally propose KITSUNE, a two-stage routing method that combines easily dispatchable L1 micro-buckets with gradient-informed offline stitching. On a weaker base the routed mixture matches the best single-stage L1 result, while individual experts become substantially more specialized; raising the micro-bucket factor past four per expert stops helping.
